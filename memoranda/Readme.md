Related Approaches and Selected Bibliography
The following works provide related perspectives on addiction, relapse, impaired control, and recovery. They include Bayesian and Active Inference accounts, nonlinear dynamical models, latent-state models, and theories of competing decision processes.

The present project investigates their relationship to a framework combining continuous affective-cognitive states, discrete psychological operating regimes, regime-dependent policy repertoires, and planning over relapse and recovery trajectories. Inclusion here indicates conceptual or mathematical relevance, rather than equivalence to the proposed framework. This is a selected bibliography, not an exhaustive review.

Active Inference and Bayesian Accounts
Schwartenbeck, P., FitzGerald, T. H. B., Mathys, C., Dolan, R., Wurst, F., Kronbichler, M., & Friston, K. (2015). [Optimal inference with suboptimal models: Addiction and active Bayesian inference](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mehy.2014.12.007). Medical Hypotheses, 84(2), 109–117.

Explains how maladaptive addictive choices can arise through Bayesian inference under an individual's generative model. Provides a foundation for distinguishing the inference principle from the model under which inference and action occur.

Miller, M., Kiverstein, J., & Rietveld, E. (2020). [Embodying addiction: A predictive processing account](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bandc.2019.105495). Brain and Cognition, 138, 105495.

Develops an account of addiction involving predictive processing, affect, and the wider person-environment relationship. Particularly relevant to the narrowing of meaningful opportunities for action and the displacement of alternative pursuits.

Kinley, I., Amlung, M., & Becker, S. (2022). [Pathologies of precision: A Bayesian account of goals, habits, and episodic foresight in addiction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bandc.2022.105843). Brain and Cognition, 158, 105843.

Proposes that differences in the precision of habitual and goal-directed reward predictions shift behavioral control toward habit. Discusses episodic future thinking as a possible means of strengthening goal-directed control.

Decision Processes and Changes in Behavioral Control
Bernheim, B. D., & Rangel, A. (2004). [Addiction and cue-triggered decision processes](https://doi.org/10.1257/0002828043052222). American Economic Review, 94(5), 1558–1590.

Presents a mathematical model distinguishing deliberative “cold” and cue-triggered “hot” decision modes. Individuals can understand their susceptibility and take actions that affect the probability of future loss of control. This is an important comparator for regime-dependent decision-making.

Redish, A. D., Jensen, S., & Johnson, A. (2008). [A unified framework for addiction: Vulnerabilities in the decision process](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X0800472X). Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 31(4), 415–437.

Integrates planning, habit, and situation-recognition systems into an account of multiple vulnerabilities underlying addictive behavior. Relevant to impaired evaluation of consequences, changes in behavioral control, and heterogeneous pathways into addiction and relapse.

Nonlinear Relapse Dynamics and Latent States
Witkiewitz, K., & Marlatt, G. A. (2007). [Modeling the complexity of post-treatment drinking: It's a rocky road to relapse](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2007.01.002). Clinical Psychology Review, 27(6), 724–738.

Treats relapse as a dynamic process and presents catastrophe-model analyses of alcohol-treatment outcomes. Relevant to abrupt behavioral transitions, interacting vulnerabilities, and feedback following a lapse.

Shirley, K. E., Small, D. S., Lynch, K. G., Maisto, S. A., & Oslin, D. W. (2010). [Hidden Markov models for alcoholism treatment trial data](https://doi.org/10.1214/09-AOAS282). The Annals of Applied Statistics, 4(1), 366–395.

Uses hierarchical Bayesian Markov and hidden Markov models to characterize changing drinking patterns and treatment effects. Provides an empirical precedent for latent-state inference and probabilistic transitions in addiction research.

Mao, S., Chou, T., & D’Orsogna, M. R. (2024). [A probabilistic model of relapse in drug addiction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mbs.2024.109184). Mathematical Biosciences, 372, 109184.

Models relapse probability through affective dynamics, stressors, cue-evoked memories, positive experiences, and substance availability. Particularly relevant to the interaction of emotional distress, remembered reward, and relapse risk.

Recent Stochastic Control Work
Aissi, D., Ciotir, I., Goreac, D., & Li, J. (2026). [Stochastic control of addiction with state-dependent jump relapse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24099). arXiv:2607.24099.

Studies addiction using a piecewise-deterministic Markov process with state-dependent relapse and recovery jumps. Develops Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations and optimal-control results. This is a related control-theoretic approach with a different state representation and objective from the present Active Inference framework.
